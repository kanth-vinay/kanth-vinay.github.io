Learning goals

  • Describe the shape, center, spread, and position of data from a graph, and identify unusual features.
  • Compare two or more data sets using the mean, median, IQR, and standard deviation.
  • Decide whether the mean or the median better describes the center of a data set.
  • Identify outliers, explain their effect, and choose the most appropriate measures of center and spread.
  • Build two-way tables, interpret relative frequencies in context, and recognize associations.
  • Recognize patterns between two numerical variables and model them with a line where a line fits.

1.3 Shape and Unusual Features

When you describe a distribution, give its shape, its center, its spread, and any unusual features: clusters, gaps, or outliers.

A distribution is skewed right when its long tail points toward the larger values, and skewed left when the tail points toward the smaller values. The direction of skew is the direction of the tail, not the direction of the peak.

2.1 From MAD to Standard Deviation

Both measures answer the same question: on average, how far are the values from the mean?

The mean absolute deviation (MAD) averages the distances from the mean. The standard deviation squares each deviation, averages the squares, and takes the square root. Squaring makes large deviations count much more than small ones.

In symbols, for n values with mean x̄: MAD = Σ|x − x̄| ÷ n, and σ = √( Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ n ).

  3. Can σ ever be smaller than the MAD? Test a few data sets and state what you find.

2.3 Outliers and the Fence Rule

An outlier is a value that falls far from the rest of the data. The 1.5 × IQR rule gives a precise test.

Lower fence = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR. Upper fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. A value below the lower fence or above the upper fence is an outlier.

A measure is resistant if a single extreme value cannot move it very much.

Problem 2.3aBy hand

Return to the reading data from Problem 1.5a:

15, 20, 20, 25, 30, 30, 30, 35, 40, 45, 60, 110
  1. Find both fences. Identify any outliers.
  2. Remove the outlier. Recalculate the mean and the median. Which changed, and by how much?
  3. Should the outlier be removed from the data? Explain when it is and is not reasonable to remove an outlier.
Problem 2.3bCalculator

3.2 Relative Frequencies

Counts are hard to compare when groups have different sizes. Relative frequencies divide by a total so the groups can be compared fairly.

  • Joint relative frequency: a cell divided by the grand total.
  • Marginal relative frequency: a row or column total divided by the grand total.
  • Conditional relative frequency: a cell divided by its row total (or its column total). It answers questions of the form "among students who ..., what fraction ...?"
Problem 3.2aBy hand

Use your completed table from 3.1. Write each answer as a fraction and as a percent.

  1. What fraction of all 40 students keep a phone in the bedroom and slept 8+ hours?
  2. What fraction of all students slept 8+ hours?
  3. Among students with a phone in the bedroom, what fraction slept 8+ hours? Among students without one?
  4. Among students who slept 8+ hours, what fraction keep a phone in the bedroom?
Problem 3.2bWritten
  1. Is there an association between keeping a phone in the bedroom and sleeping 8+ hours? Support your answer with conditional relative frequencies.

3.4 Scatter Plots

A scatter plot shows two numerical variables measured on the same individuals. Describe it by direction, form, and strength, and note any outliers.

  • Direction: positive (both increase together), negative (one increases as the other decreases), or none.
  • Form: linear (points follow a line) or nonlinear (points follow a curve).
  • Strength: how closely the points follow the form.

Tools

Conventions

Different textbooks and calculators make slightly different choices. This unit uses the following.

Quartiles

Q1 is the median of the lower half of the ordered data, and Q3 is the median of the upper half. When the number of values is odd, the median itself is left out of both halves. Some calculators use a different method and may give slightly different quartiles.

Standard deviation

This unit uses σ, which divides by n. Calculators also report s, which divides by n − 1 and is used when the data is a sample from a larger population. On a TI calculator σ appears as σx and s appears as Sx. In Desmos, stdevp(list) gives σ and stdev(list) gives s.

Outliers

A value is an outlier if it is less than Q1 − 1.5 × IQR or greater than Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. In box plots, whiskers extend to the smallest and largest values that are not outliers, and outliers are drawn as separate points.

Rounding

Round final answers to two decimal places unless a problem says otherwise. Keep full precision in intermediate steps.
